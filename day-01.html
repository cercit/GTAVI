gta v

The game that kept earning for 13 years. It came out in September 2013, and it sold another five million copies in the last quarter alone, right before its own sequel.

01what it is, who it's for

A crime story set in Los Santos, a fake Los Angeles, where you switch between three criminals. Inside the same box sits GTA Online, a shared world where you build your own character and play with others.

That one product serves very different people. Story players who finish the campaign and move on. Online regulars who grind heists and collect cars for years. Role-players who treat the map as a place to live a second life. And upgraders who bought it on PS3, then again on PS4, then again on PS5.

02the problem it solved

For players: one game that was a driving game, a shooter, a heist movie and a hangout spot at once. When new content came, it came free, so friends were never split by who had paid for which pack.

For Rockstar: the big-game problem. You spend years and a fortune on one release, sales spike for a few months, then fall away. GTA V broke that cycle. It kept selling, and the online side kept people spending long after launch.

03how it makes money

Three ways, stacked on top of each other:

  • The boxSold once, then sold again on every new console. Same game, new buyers each generation.
  • Online spendingShark Cards (in-game money for real money) and later the GTA+ subscription. Days 6 and 7.
  • Staying powerEvery year it stays popular, Rockstar keeps players' accounts, attention and habits ready for the next game.

05what i'd change

In 2013 Rockstar said story add-ons for GTA V were coming. They never came. Every new thing after that went into GTA Online, because that's where the money was. Commercially it was the right call, and the numbers prove it. But a big group of players bought GTA V for the story, and for 13 years the product quietly stopped being built for them.

As a PM, I'd have kept one small team on the story side. Not for revenue, but because those players are exactly who buys GTA VI on day one, and GTA VI is single-player at launch.

PM concept: the cash cow

In the BCG matrix, a cash cow is a product that earns far more than it costs to keep alive, and pays for the company's next bets. GTA V is about the cleanest example you'll find. It paid for Red Dead Redemption 2 and for 13 years of GTA VI.

The hard question for any PM: when do you stop milking the cow and start feeding the next one? Rockstar's answer seems to be: never fully. GTA Online stays supported even after GTA VI launches.
