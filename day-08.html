01

Banned, then bought

The same company, eight years apart, about the same mod.

11 Aug 2015
"The FiveM project is an unauthorized alternate multiplayer service that contains code designed to facilitate piracy."

Rockstar, explaining why the people behind FiveM had their Social Club accounts suspended.

11 Aug 2023
"Cfx.re, the team behind the FiveM and RedM projects – is now officially a part of Rockstar Games!"

Cfx.re's own announcement. A month later it was blunter: "Rockstar Games has acquired Cfx.re."

PC Gamer called it "a remarkable turnaround". The price was never disclosed.

02

Who it's for

FiveM isn't GTA Online. It's the software that lets anyone host their own version of Los Santos, and most of what people call "GTA roleplay" runs on it. Four groups use it.

Player

Lives a second life

Joins a server to be a cop, a medic, a mechanic or a gang member, with a character that lasts for months. Wants a city, not a mission list.

Owner

Runs the city

Hosts a server, writes its rules and pays for more slots. Default is 48 players; the top plan lists up to 2,048.

Creator

Builds the pieces

Writes scripts, maps, cars and clothes in Lua, JavaScript or C#, and since January 2026 can sell them on Cfx's own marketplace.

Streamer

Puts on a show

Roleplay is built for watching. Rockstar paid GTA Online items to people who watched roleplay streams on Twitch in September 2026.

03

The problem it solved

For players

GTA Online is one shared Rockstar service with Rockstar's missions, economy and rules. A community can't run its own server, invent its own jobs or keep a story going there.

FiveM is that missing layer: custom multiplayer next to GTA Online, not inside it. Rockstar's own 2015 words describe it well, "an alternate multiplayer service".

For Rockstar

A large PC audience was playing GTA on servers Rockstar didn't run. First Rockstar treated that as piracy (2015), then set rules and let roleplay continue (2022), then bought the platform (2023).

Buying it turned the biggest unofficial GTA community into a Rockstar product, with Rockstar setting the rules.

> connect cfx.re/join

"The FiveM business is a profitable business for us."

Strauss Zelnick, Take-Two's CEO, on the earnings call of 9 November 2026. No figure given.

05

How it makes money

  • Server plansDirectPlaying is free. Server owners who want more than 48 players pay for Element Club: Argentum at $15 a month, Aurum at $25 and Platinum at $50, plus VAT. More slots, custom clothing, and on the top plan an animated icon and a highlighted spot in the server list.
  • A cut of server storesDirectServers sell priority queues, cosmetics and perks through Tebex. Tebex charges 15% on FiveM sales, against 5% for other games. How that fee is shared with Rockstar isn't public.
  • The creator marketplaceDirectSince January 2026, creators sell scripts, maps and props on the Cfx Marketplace. IGN found bundles at "upwards of $450". Rockstar's share isn't public.
  • GTA V and GTA OnlineIndirectFiveM needs a PC copy of GTA V, a game Rockstar sells. And roleplay now feeds GTA Online: watching NoPixel V streams in September 2026 earned up to GTA$1.5 million in GTA Online.

What people complain about

Complaint

Piracy, then a purchase

In 2015 Rockstar called FiveM piracy and banned its makers. In 2023 it bought them. Some modders still remember the first part.

Complaint

The rules changed

Rockstar's 2022 rules listed selling things on roleplay servers as a problem. By 2026, its own platform runs a paid marketplace.

Complaint

Paid scripts that break

On Cfx's own forum, server owners complain that locked paid scripts stay broken once a seller stops updating them, and that cheaters are still a problem.

Complaint

One door left

Rival mod services are closing. RAGE:MP's servers were told to move to FiveM, which leaves less choice for server owners.

09

What I'd change

Buying FiveM was the right call. Players had already built the roleplay scene Rockstar never made, and owning the platform turned a legal risk into a business. The friction now is trust.

As a PM, I'd do three things. Publish one clear rulebook for what servers can sell, so owners aren't guessing which 2022 rule still applies. Make the 15% fee on FiveM stores easier to understand, or bring it closer to the 5% other games pay. And give server owners a clear answer on what happens to their cities when GTA VI comes to PC.

PM concept · Learn this

Platforms and user-generated content

A product is something you build for users. A platform is somewhere users build for each other. With user-generated content, the players make the experiences, and the platform earns from plans, fees and a store.

FiveM's players built cities, jobs and stories that Rockstar never had to make. By buying the platform, Rockstar earns from the servers that run them and gets to set the rules, down to which rival services keep running.

The question for any PM: why buy a mod you once called piracy? Because the community was already building more roleplay than Rockstar could. Owning the platform turns that community from a risk into revenue.
