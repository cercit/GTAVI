Rockstar Games Launcher

A free Windows app, released on 17 September 2019, that put Rockstar's PC games, its shop, updates and cloud saves in one place. It's also how Rockstar sells to PC players without handing a store its cut.

02

The problem it solved

For players: a Rockstar PC collection used to be split across Steam, old discs and a separate Rockstar account. The launcher promised one shelf: find your games, keep them updated, sync your saves.

For Rockstar: every game sold on someone else's store pays that store a share. Steam's standard cut is 30%. A store of your own means you keep the sale, you know the buyer, and you can run your own offers. Rockstar never published a strategy note saying this, but its first big move was clear: Red Dead Redemption 2 PC pre-orders were launcher-only for two weeks.

Rockstar didn't leave Steam. It made sure every road still led back to its own app.

05

What I'd change

The launcher's job is to own the PC relationship. The problem is where it sits: between people and games they already paid for. A Steam copy of GTA V still has to start through it. So every time it broke, at the Red Dead Redemption 2 launch, during the 2021 maintenance, it stopped people playing games they owned, including offline single-player ones.

As a PM, I'd earn the install instead of forcing it. Let single-player games bought elsewhere start without it. Make it worth opening: the 2026 overlay, with chat, Discord friends and Streamer Mode, is exactly that kind of reason. And design every outage so that offline play keeps working.

Distribution, and the store cut

Distribution is how a product reaches its customers. Big stores like Steam bring you an audience, and charge for it: 30% of each sale, less at high volumes. Epic charges 12%. Your own store keeps nearly everything, but only works if people already want what you sell enough to install your app.

Rockstar has that pull, so the launcher makes sense. But it never went launcher-only: Red Dead Redemption 2 reached Steam a month later, and Red Dead Redemption launched on Steam and Epic too. Reach still matters.

The question for any PM: is it worth annoying some users to keep the store fee? Only if your product is strong enough that they come anyway, and only if you keep the doors open on the big stores too.
