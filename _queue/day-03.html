RAGERockstar Advanced Game Engine

The one Rockstar product you can't buy. It's the engine underneath GTA IV, Red Dead Redemption, GTA V and Red Dead Redemption 2, and no studio outside Rockstar has ever been allowed to use it.

01 · Geometry

What it is, who it's for

A game engine is the toolkit underneath a game. It draws the world, moves the cars, makes people fall when they're hit, plays the sound, and loads the next street before you reach it. Most studios rent one. Rockstar wrote its own and called it RAGE.

Its real users are inside the company: Rockstar's ten studios, who all build on the same base. Players feel it without ever seeing the name. And outside studios get nothing, because RAGE has never been licensed.

02 · Lighting

The problem it solved

GTA III, Vice City and San Andreas ran on an engine called RenderWare, made by a company called Criterion. In July 2004, Electronic Arts bought Criterion. Overnight, the tech under Rockstar's biggest series belonged to its biggest rival.

Rockstar's co-founder Jamie King later recalled being told that, because EA now owned RenderWare, Rockstar had to make its own engine. It already had a head start: Take-Two had bought Angel Studios in 2002 and renamed it Rockstar San Diego. The new engine was first tested on something small, a table-tennis game in 2006, to prove the physics. GTA IV followed two years later.

Its first game was table tennis. Its biggest, GTA V, has sold nearly 235 million copies.

03 · Shadows

How it makes money

It doesn't, directly. RAGE has no price and no customers. Its value sits in the shadows, in what it saves and what it makes possible.

What it saves: the most popular engine for sale, Unreal, charges 5% of a game's gross revenue after the first $1 million. On a game GTA V's size, that is a very large cheque every year, for as long as the game sells. Rockstar pays nobody.

What it makes possible: every improvement carries into the next game. Red Dead Redemption 2's clouds and fog were built as real volumes of air, and in 2025 GTA V on PC got ray-traced lighting, twelve years after launch.

06 · Post-process

What I'd change

Building RAGE was the right call. But owning your engine means owning every problem too. GTA IV's PC version had a rough launch in 2008, and Red Dead Redemption 2 on PC stuttered in 2019. With a bought engine, a lot of that work is shared with thousands of other studios. With your own, it's all yours.

The bigger cost is time. When the engine has to grow for every new game, the game waits for the engine. Rockstar seems to know this:

"The engine, the tools and the team we've built during this project will allow us to do things more quickly."Phil Hooker, Rockstar, to Bloomberg, October 2026

As a PM, I'd treat the tools as a product of their own, with the internal studios as its customers. Measure how long it takes a team to go from idea to playable, and fund the engine on that number, not just on how good the next trailer looks.

PM concept · Learn this

Build vs buy

Every product team faces it: build the thing yourself, or pay for one that exists. Building gives control and no ongoing fees, but you carry all the cost and all the bugs. Buying is faster to start, but you follow someone else's roadmap and pay them for as long as you use it.

Build

Rockstar · RAGE
  • No royalty, ever
  • Shaped for one thing: huge open worlds
  • A rival can't buy it out from under you
  • Years of work before the first game
  • Every bug and every new console is your job
  • New hires must learn a private tool

Buy

Most studios · Unreal Engine
  • Start building on day one
  • Huge pool of people who already know it
  • Thousands of studios share the fixes
  • 5% of gross revenue after the first $1 million
  • Their roadmap, not yours
  • Same tools as everyone else

It isn't one-way. CD Projekt Red, which built The Witcher 3 and Cyberpunk 2077 on its own engine, announced in 2022 that its next games would move to Unreal Engine 5.

The question for any PM: build when the thing is what makes you different and you have the scale to pay for it. Buy when it's plumbing. For Rockstar, the engine is the product.
