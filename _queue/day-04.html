01

Who it's for

Rockstar announced it in March 2008 as a home for GTA IV "and all future Rockstar Games titles". Four kinds of players ended up depending on it.

L

Long-haul GTA Online players

Their character, money and cars have to survive every new console. The account is how they move.

C

Crews

Since Max Payne 3 in 2012, a crew made in one game carries into the next, including GTA Online.

S

Stat chasers

Leaderboards, awards and 100% progress, the reason Social Club existed in 2008.

P

PC players

On PC the account is required, and since 2019 it comes with Rockstar's own launcher.

02

The problem it solved

For players: your progress shouldn't belong to one console. In March 2022, when GTA V moved to PS5 and Xbox Series, players brought their GTA Online characters, money, cars, properties and the jobs they had made, through this account.

For Rockstar: without it, every player relationship belongs to Sony, Microsoft or Steam. With it, Rockstar knows its players directly, can reward them across games, link partner offers like Twitch Prime, and hang a subscription like GTA+ on the same login.

The game is what you play. The account is who you are in it.

03

How it makes money

It's free, and Take-Two has never published a user count or revenue for it. The account earns by connecting things that do earn.

  • GTA+: a monthly subscription attached to the same login
  • The PC store: the 2019 launcher sells games directly
  • Partners: link Twitch Prime and other services for rewards

06

What I'd change

Folding Social Club into one Rockstar Games account was a good decision. One brand, one login, less confusion before the biggest launch Rockstar has ever had.

The way it happened is what I'd change. Players noticed the name was gone before Rockstar said anything; the support article explaining it came months later. Then, by most reports, the old profile pages with years of screenshots and videos stopped showing. For a product whose whole job is to hold your identity, that's the one thing it shouldn't drop quietly.

As a PM, I'd ship a rename like a launch: say it first, say what stays the same, and give people a way to download what they made before anything goes away.

Network effects, and retiring a brand

Network effects mean a product gets more valuable as more people use it. One shared account means your friends, crew and progress are already waiting in Rockstar's next game, which makes the next game easier to start and harder to leave.

Retiring a brand is the other half. Companies choose between a "house of brands", many separate names, and a "branded house", one parent name on everything. Rockstar moved from the first to the second.

House of brands

Each product has its own name and identity. Flexible, but the names compete for attention.

Rockstar before 2023: Social Club + Launcher + GTA+

Branded house

One parent name on everything. Simpler for customers, and every product builds the same brand.

Rockstar after 2023: a Rockstar Games account

The question for any PM: when a product's name is famous, is retiring it worth the confusion? Here the answer was yes. The account mattered more than the label.
