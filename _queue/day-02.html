GTA Online

The free mode that turned a game into a business. It launched two weeks after GTA V, cost nothing extra, and has been updated for free for 13 years.

01

Who it's for

You make your own character in the GTA V map and play in sessions of up to 30 players. What you do there depends on who you are.

Empire builders run businesses, buy property and grind for money. Heist crews want four friends, a plan and a big payout. Car collectors buy, tune and show off. Racers and fighters want quick matches and bragging rights. And since 2022, newcomers can buy GTA Online on its own, without the story mode.

02

The problem it solved

For players: what do we do after the story ends? GTA Online gave them a world that keeps going, with progress that carries over and new things to do every few weeks. And because updates were free, friends never got split up over who had bought which map pack.

For Rockstar: the revenue cliff. A big single-player game sells most of its copies in the first few months, then slows down. GTA Online kept people playing, and spending, for years after launch.

Every big update in 13 years was free. The money came from somewhere else.

03

How it makes money

  • Shark CardsReal money for in-game money, from $4.99 up to $99.99 for GTA$10 million. You can earn the same cash by playing. Shark Cards are for people who'd rather not wait.
  • GTA+A monthly membership since March 2022. $5.99 at launch, $7.99 from April 2024. Members get GTA$500,000 every month plus cars and perks.
  • The boxEvery copy of GTA V includes GTA Online, so every new player is also a possible Shark Card buyer. The game keeps the box selling, and the box keeps the game full of players.

05

What I'd change

The most common complaint, from players and the press, is about prices. New cars and businesses kept getting more expensive, while the pay for older missions mostly stayed the same. So a new player looks at a GTA$5 million car and the fastest way there is a Shark Card.

As a PM I'd treat the in-game economy like a real one. When prices go up, payouts should follow, at least for the missions new players do first. Rockstar has done some of this: Cayo Perico can be played alone, and the standalone version gives new players a starting boost. I'd make that the rule, not the exception, because the player who quits in week one never buys anything at all.

Live ops and lifetime value

Lifetime value is how much one customer spends over their whole time with your product. A game sold once has a ceiling: the box price. Live ops means running the product after launch with events, updates and offers, so people keep coming back. Every return visit is another chance to earn.

The question for any PM: why do free updates earn more than paid ones? Because a paid update is a gate. Some players buy it, the rest drift away. A free update brings everyone back into the same world, and the ones who want to go faster pay for it.
